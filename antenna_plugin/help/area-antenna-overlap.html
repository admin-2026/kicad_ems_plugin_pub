Antenna overlaps copper in the area

The antenna's own copper — the candidates the scan you started splices, or the one being placed — lands on metal that already exists on the feed layer inside the area rectangle. The warning names the spot in board coordinates, so you can go and look at it in the editor. This is a warning, not a blocker.

Why it matters

The scan splices each candidate's copper into the feed layer's plotted gerbers and simulates the result. That splice assumes the copper lands on empty board: existing metal under the antenna — a track, a zone fill, a pad, or an antenna footprint placed by an earlier run — simply merges with the candidate. Nothing errors; the simulated antenna is a different shape from the intended one, and every number that comes back is quietly wrong. A placed footprint whose copper crosses existing metal shorts to it on the fabricated board the same way.

What is checked — and what deliberately isn't

Only the antenna's copper inside the area rectangle is tested, against the copper layer the feed uses. Outside the area the feed stub and a ground short are supposed to overlap the surrounding ground pour — that is how the port references ground — so nothing outside the rectangle is judged, and a pour flush with the area's edge does not count as inside it.

The same goes for the first millimetre inside the feed edge: the antenna's pins sit exactly on that edge, and the area is normally placed hanging slightly over the ground pour so they reach it. Copper that close to the feed edge is the connection, not an obstruction, and is not judged — everything deeper is (a pour flooding the whole area still warns).

And nothing is checked until you press Start scan or Generate grids: that is when copper is spliced into the feed layer, so that is when there is something to overlap. Sliding the preview around beforehand cannot raise this warning. Once a pass has started, the check covers every candidate of the sweep — they reach differently far, so a clear preview never meant a clear scan — and it is re-run against the live board, so it clears as soon as you move the copper out of the way.

How to fix it

  1. Move the area marker (M) so the antenna's trace runs over empty board on the feed layer.
  2. Or clear the copper it crosses: reroute the track, add a keep-out to the zone and re-pour, or delete a leftover antenna footprint from an earlier wizard run (the preview drawn on the marker itself is fine — it is ignored, and the scan strips it before plotting).
  3. Or shrink/reshape the sweep (the scan sliders and its min/max) so the candidates stay clear of the metal, and scan again.
Why it's only a warning: you may know better — the overlapping metal might be exactly the copper you intend the antenna to connect to, or something you are about to remove. The warning describes the pass you last started; it stays until the next one, so after clearing the copper you will see it go on the next banner refresh.
