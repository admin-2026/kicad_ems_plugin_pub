Candidates don't fit the area

Some of the candidates the scan was asked for describe an antenna the area marker cannot hold. Those are skipped — they are never spliced, meshed or simulated — and the pass runs the ones that fit. This is a warning, not a blocker: the scan still runs, just with fewer candidates than you asked for.

Why a candidate doesn't fit

Every candidate is laid out inside the marked rectangle, starting at the feed arrow and keeping a small border off the other three edges. A candidate overflows when there is no way to draw it in there: the resonant length is longer than the stem plus the room along the edge, the stem itself is deeper than the rectangle, the track is wider than the feed edge is long, or the topology's own constraints (an inverted-F's tap inside its arm) cannot be met at that size. The design says which in the warning.

Why they are skipped rather than simulated

There is no antenna to measure. A geometry that doesn't fit has no copper to splice into the gerbers, so the alternative to skipping is a solver run per miss that ends in the same message several hours later. Skipping is also what keeps the rest of the sweep honest: the candidates that do fit are simulated and scored exactly as they would have been.

The warning exists because the skipping is otherwise invisible. A sweep of five candidates that quietly runs two is not the scan you planned — the resonance you are hunting may be entirely outside what is left.

The two shapes this takes

  • A bounded sweep (you typed min and max on the swept row): the values that don't fit are named, in millimetres. The rest of the range runs.
  • The automatic λ/4 ladder (both bounds on the resonant length left blank): nothing is dropped by name. The ladder's rungs are clamped to the longest antenna the area holds, so the ones past that ceiling land on the same length and collapse into one candidate. Asking for five gets you however many distinct lengths the area has room for.

How to fix it

  1. Grow the area marker on the board: double-click it to enter its group and drag a corner of the rectangle. Deeper gives the stem more room; longer along the feed edge gives the arm more room.
  2. Or narrow the sweep to values that fit: lower the swept row's max, or lower the fixed values (a narrower track leaves more length for the antenna, and so does a longer stem — up to the depth of the area, past which the stem is what stops fitting).
  3. Or lower the target frequency — a quarter wave at a lower frequency is longer, so it is the same fix in reverse: if the ladder collapses, the antenna you want simply doesn't fit the space you gave it.
Tip: drag the swept row's slider before starting a pass. A candidate that doesn't fit is still drawn — on the marker's own layer instead of on copper, spilling out of the rectangle by exactly what is missing — so you can see how much bigger the area needs to be, and in which direction.
