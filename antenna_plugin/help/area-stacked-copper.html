Copper stacked over the area

Another copper layer carries copper directly over or under the antenna area. The antenna sits on the feed layer; a plane on a layer stacked behind it detunes or kills it. This is a warning, not a blocker.

Why it matters

A monopole needs open space on the side it radiates into — including the layers above and below it in the stackup, on the far side of the board. A ground pour (or any large copper) there behaves like a shield close behind the radiator: it shifts the resonant frequency and can suppress radiation altogether. The simulation models the real stackup, so the built board and the simulation will at least agree — but both may be detuned.

How to fix it

  1. Add a keep-out over the antenna area on the other copper layer(s) named in the warning, and re-pour any zones there.
  2. Or move the antenna area (M) to a part of the board with no plane behind it — typically out past the edge of the ground pour.
  3. On a multi-layer board, confirm the layer stacked behind the feed layer is the one you intend to keep clear.
Why it's only a warning: the area rectangle is a hint, not the antenna, and the copper behind it may be intentional (or clear where the trace actually runs). The check tests the rectangle against every other copper layer — a conservative overlap that's worth confirming.
