Feed edge has no ground

There is no copper on the feed layer just outside the feed edge of the antenna area — the edge the feed triangle sits on. The feed port drives the antenna against ground, so it needs a ground pour to reference right there. This is a warning, not a blocker.

Why it matters

The simulator runs a short stub out through the feed edge to reach the ground pour, and the port measures the antenna's impedance relative to that pour. With no copper adjacent to the feed edge (for example, the feed edge flush with the board outline, or the pour set back too far), the port has nothing to reference and the simulated impedance is meaningless.

How to fix it

  1. In the PCB editor, move the area marker (M) so its feed edge sits against the ground pour — the copper the antenna will feed from.
  2. Or rotate it (R) so the feed triangle points in from the grounded side rather than off the board.
  3. If the pour simply doesn't reach the feed edge yet, extend it (or its keep-out) up to the edge and re-pour.
Why it's only a warning: the area rectangle is a hint, not the antenna — you may have a ground reference the checker can't see, or be placing the pour next. The check looks a couple of millimetres outside the feed edge on the feed layer; a clear result there is worth a second look, not proof of a mistake.
