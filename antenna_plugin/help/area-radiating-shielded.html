Radiating edge is shielded

There is copper on the feed layer just outside the radiating edge — the edge opposite the feed. The monopole radiates off that side, so it should face open space or the board edge. Copper behind it acts as a shield. This is a warning, not a blocker.

Why it matters

A monopole works by radiating into free space off its far end. When that end is backed by metal (a ground pour, another trace, a nearby plane), the field is reflected and absorbed instead of radiated: the antenna can still look reasonable in an isolated simulation yet fail to work on the built board. The extreme case is an area boxed in by copper on every edge — nowhere to radiate.

How to fix it

  1. Move the area marker (M) so its radiating edge sits at the board edge, with open space beyond it.
  2. Or rotate it (R) so the feed enters from the copper side and the antenna points away from the pour.
  3. Pull back or cut the copper behind the radiating edge (a keep-out on the pour over the antenna area keeps it clear).
Why it's only a warning: the area rectangle is a conservative superset of where the trace actually goes, and the copper the checker sees may be intentional or far enough not to matter. The check probes a couple of millimetres outside the radiating edge on the feed layer — a reason to look, not a verdict.
